# 2. evidence of relevance (preliminary)

The preliminary relevance of the proposed platform is supported by research on product lifecycle information management and digital product passports. 

Existing studies, particularly in the electronics sector, identify challenges related to information availability, lifecycle traceability, and data exchange between participants. These findings provide an initial basis for investigating a product lifecycle tracking platform. However, their applicability to other product categories requires further examination. 

The team will investigate potential application scenarios and user needs before determining the product categories and processes to be supported by the prototype.

| source | relevant finding |
|---|---|
| wagner (2025) | identifies information gaps affecting the repair, reuse, and recycling of electronic products. |
| chaudhuri et al. (2025) | highlights data exchange, implementation effort, and information security as challenges for digital product passport adoption. |
| vilchez et al. (2025) | demonstrates a digital product passport prototype for second-hand electronic devices. |
___

These studies suggest potential value in maintaining accessible lifecycle records for individual products.

Next steps: interviews with potential users and further analysis of existing systems are planned to identify actual difficulties, select a primary application scenario, and refine the project requirements.

Note: most concentrate on dpp and electronics, so the review may be broadened after further selections.
